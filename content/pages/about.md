# About Me

I'm Gary, a data engineer with a growing interest in what happens beyond the technology, thinking about people, strategy and everything else involved in creating a great data culture and team.

I've spent more than 15 years working with data across healthcare, energy and insurance, starting with databases, SQL and data warehousing and gradually moving into data engineering, technical leadership and people management.

Today I work as a Lead Data Engineer in the NHS, where I lead development of data warehouse processing supporting NHS dentistry and ophthalmic reporting. A large part of my current work involves modernising established data platforms and moving from Oracle and traditional reporting technologies towards Microsoft Fabric, Power BI and more modern approaches to data engineering.

I still enjoy the technical side of the job. I like solving difficult data problems, understanding how systems work and finding ways to make processes more reliable and efficient. I also increasingly getting enjoyment from the leadership and mentoring side of the job, which I find very rewarding. 

## From data engineering to data leadership

My career has largely been built around getting data from one place to another and making it useful. That and making sure it is accurate, timely and all that jazz. 

I've worked across the full data lifecycle: transactional systems, ingestion, transformation, data modelling, warehousing, reporting, analysis, data quality and deployment. I've designed ETL processes, worked with complex databases, automated manual processes and helped build data platforms from the ground up.

I've also spent a significant part of my career working between technical teams and the people who actually need the data.

That has taught me that the difficult part of a data project isn't always the technology.

Sometimes it's agreeing what something means.

Sometimes it's understanding what someone actually needs rather than what they initially ask for.

Sometimes it's getting different people to agree on ownership.

Sometimes the technically correct solution simply isn't the most useful solution.

Those are the problems I find increasingly interesting.

Over the last few years I've deliberately started developing the leadership side of my career as well. I've led projects, allocated work, supported the development of other engineers and taken on line-management responsibilities. This has included pay and performance reviews, dealing with personal situations and conflict and managing expectations of a variety of stakeholders. 

I've also completed leadership development through the NHS and the Carruthers and Jackson Data Leaders Summer School, where I explored areas including data maturity, data contracts and data strategy.

I'm still very much learning.

That's part of the reason for this site.

## What I know

My strongest technical background is in data engineering and data warehousing.

SQL has been a constant throughout my career, alongside Oracle, SQL Server, Python and a range of ETL and reporting technologies. More recently I've been working extensively with Microsoft Fabric, PySpark and Power BI as part of the modernisation of our data platform.

I've worked with both new systems and mature environments, and I particularly enjoy the problems that appear when the two worlds meet.

I'm interested in:

* Data engineering and modern data platforms
* Data architecture and modelling
* Data quality
* Data governance
* Metadata
* Data strategy
* Data maturity
* Automation
* Reporting and analytics
* Technical leadership
* The relationship between technology and the people who use it

I'm also interested in learning new technologies rather than treating my current toolkit as fixed. A number of my home projects have been deliberately chosen to explore technologies and approaches outside my day job. I have played with Snowflake, dbt, databricks and built bespoke ETLs for "fun". These projects have been made much easier through the use of AI, an amazing tool when used correctly. 

## Leadership doesn't only happen at work

Some of my most useful leadership experience has come from outside my job.

I've been involved in running and coaching within the local running community for many years and have been the Event Director of Eastbourne junior parkrun since 2020.

That role has involved much more than turning up on a Sunday morning.

I've had to build and maintain volunteer teams, budget, train people, manage risk, deal with difficult situations, communicate with children, parents and volunteers, work with external stakeholders and occasionally make unpopular decisions.

I also have a long-standing involvement with the British Dupuytren's Society, including communicating scientific and patient research to audiences ranging from patients to medical professionals. I've had the opportunity to present research internationally and work on projects involving the collection and analysis of patient opinions. I am hoping to take on some more data work within the charity to further my strategic knowledge and skills. 

These experiences have given me a perspective that doesn't come entirely from working as a technical data professional. 

## Why this blog?

I created this site because I want somewhere to think out loud about data.

Some posts will be technical.

Some will be about data strategy, governance or maturity.

Some will be reflections on leadership.

Others will probably start with a book, a project or a problem I've encountered and end somewhere I didn't expect.

Technology matters. Architecture matters. Good engineering matters. But so do people, processes, ownership, culture, communication and leadership. I'm interested in how all of those things fit together.

I don't claim to have all the answers. In fact, one of the reasons I'm writing is to work through some of the questions myself.

My background gives me a fairly technical perspective on data. I'm now trying to broaden that perspective and understand the bigger picture.

So this blog is partly a record of that journey.

And hopefully, occasionally, something I've learned along the way will be useful to someone else and worst case it acts as a reminder for me. 

## Outside of data

When I'm not working with data, you'll usually find me with my family or involved in running or parkrun.

I'm a keen runner, coach and long-time volunteer, and I'm fascinated by the amount of useful data generated by something as simple as thousands of people turning up on Saturday mornings to run 5km.

I've built a number of small data projects around running and parkrun, partly because I enjoy them and partly because they give me a way to experiment with ideas outside of work.

I'm curious about things, I like understanding how they work, and I enjoy trying to make them better.

## Where I'm heading

I'm not trying to leave the technical side of data behind.

I think having a strong technical foundation is valuable for anyone working in data leadership because it gives you an understanding of what is actually possible, what is difficult, and where technology is being used to solve a problem that isn't really a technology problem.

What I want to develop is the ability to connect that technical understanding with strategy, people and organisational change. This site is my way of documenting the journey.
