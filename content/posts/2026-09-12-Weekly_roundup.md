Well a few things kicked off this week and I can’t share all of it. I had some concerns that I had to flag with another line manager and after some digging it looked like everything was above board but perhaps some communication on one person's part was lacking.  

Then there was a technical issue with pipelines over the weekend, these were outside of my control but we got them fixed. I think the key learning here was communicating the issue. I shared the problem with the relevant team and it appeared it was isolated to my work but then others started raising their hands as well, so we probably should have sent out some feelers earlier on in the process. 

I am trying to do some reading from the Chief Data Officers playbook to build on what I learnt on the summer school course. I am not studying it in detail or anything but just trying to take some 5 - 10 minutes chunks of time and seeing where I land in the book. Turns out I landed on the hype cycle. Nothing ground breaking but the key point I wanted to share from it, especially relevant with all the AI hype, is that data is the foundation of the information that drives everything. You need to have strong and robust data in place to be able to build the “cool” products on top of. Reminds me of a quote from ScottTaylor that “Good decisions based on bad data, are just bad decisions you don’t know about yet.”

I had the joy of doing mandatory data security training this week. I didn’t learn much, a lot of it is basic stuff that anyone would know the answer to and it is about ensuring that people are doing the basics right. As a data professional I would hope I can pass this training easily but wonder if there are ways of making it more interesting. 

I had some data issues that took up most of the rest of my week but they just went to show how important data quality and validation checks are. This issue could have gone unnoticed for weeks and landed us in real trouble when our monthly run is due, instead it was spotted instantly and we could put in a fix. That being said we got our best brains working on it and nobody can quite work out how the original issue arose, the process will be getting replaced in full within the next 6 months so lots of guardrails and extra checks will go in place to stop any future issues. 

In closing: 

I saw the following, uncredited quote and it helps to sum up a conversation I had with my team on Gen AI and the frustrations we have with how some people are using it.  

“I could spend an hour writing SQL from scratch—or let AI draft it in 30 seconds and spend 10 minutes validating every line.

​There’s a crucial distinction many miss:
​
Using AI in your work makes you efficient.
​
Using AI for your work lands you in trouble.
​
Copying and pasting syntax isn't engineering. You still have to understand what the code is doing, how it executes under the hood, and why it's structured that way.

​AI doesn't replace the developer; it sharpens the workflow. Like any tool, it’s meant to amplify expertise, not to be a  substitute for it.”
