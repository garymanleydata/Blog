## Learning as a data leader

I find it really hard to know what to learn as someone in a data leadership role. I currently do line manage and lead a small team of data engineers but this course is really geared for those looking to be a Chief Data Officer and maybe one day that will be me? 

I had heard about the course from others that have done it before and through reading books by Carruthers and Jackson. 

## About C&J

So from Amazon and looking on YouTube they are real experts. 

Caroline Carruthers is a data expert and co-founder of Carruthers and Jackson. She was one of the first women to take on the role of Chief Data Officer in the UK, I believe she thinks she is the first.

Peter Jackson is the other co-founder of Carruthers and Jackson and  has been Director, Group Data Sciences at Legal and General, Chief Data Officer at Southern Water and Head of Data at The Pensions Regulator. At this point he was already an experienced data guys with years of experience in Data Strategy, Data Technologies, Master Data Management Strategies, Data Governance Frameworks, GDPR and Data Science Strategies. 

The delivery of the course is pretty informal, a good laugh and it is clear from the start they really know what they are talking about. 

## The Course

As I said the course is aimed at data leaders and covers a variety of important topics such as data maturity, data strategy, building a data team and much more. It is a really interesting mix and for me was the first multi-day course I have been on that wasn't technical in nature. 

## Self Awareness

I think one of the important attributes the course encourages in self awareness, both of yourself and your organisation. As part of the "homework" you look into which of the qualities you are strong and less strong in and think about how you can work on them. There is talk of the hype cycle, something everyone is going through with AI at the moment. With AI there is just so much hype, don't get me wrong they are such useful tools but many are becoming too reliant on it and sometimes getting sent in circles. You are encouraged to look at the Ikigai framework from the point of view of a data leader. Who You Are, Who Do You Want to Be, What does the Market Need, and What the Market Is Prepared to Pay For.

You also need to look at what makes a great leader and most of it isn't technical. 

- Communications
- Relationship Building
- Credibility
- Specialist Knowledge
- Strategy
- Passion
- Luck

I come from a technical background and feel this helps with knowledge and credibility but all the others are a even playing field and it was interesting on the course to hear of the diverse backgrounds, then again my degree is in Molecular Genetics with 2 years of a PhD on cancer research. 

## Communication 

I think one of the biggest takeaways was communication. I don't have much to say on it myself, but Scott Taylor did a great discussion on his work and has a great book that really helps on the subject. 

## Planning

Anyone who has read their books or heard talks by C&J will have heard their idea's on the first 100 / 300 days in a CDO role. This is covered in more detail on the course. Funnily enough a lot of this phase is thinking about communication with a lot of encouragement for Coffee and Cake. Whilst I don't take coffee or cake myself but the principle is to have informal gatherings with people throughout the organisation. This also gets a mention in their book, make sure you leave time and budget to get to know people. 

## Data Maturity and Strategy 

Obviously I am only covering things at a high level so not as to use their material, you should just do the course. They cover some ways to look at data maturity and how to assess it. Their model covers 12 different areas and seems very thorough and is something they can help you with but you need to think about:

- Purpose (Where are we going?): Strategy, Risk, Governance.
- People (Who is doing the work?): Skills, Behaviour, Leadership.
- Method (How do we play the game?): Organisation, Framework, Policies.
- Tools (What do we play with?): Architecture, Metrics, Technology.

We also covered data strategy. This can be a tough one, especially for a new data entity or leader, as you are fighting to change the strategy whilst BAU operations are still taking place. The general plan was to come up with an urgent, immediate and long term target strategy. 

## People and Technology

People isn't just about the data people in your team it is about the approach of your Org and the people in your Org to data and data initiatives. Can you get people in the Org to shout and cheer for data, can you get people to adopt and accept the change you want to drive. You need to encourage a positive data culture in your company. There are lots of ways to do this and I like to think I am good at promoting what we do, how we do it and making sure things are done right. You should encourage the improvement of data literacy throughout the organisation by improving understanding.

Technology was also discussed but not in great depth, just don't mention the s word. Technology is constantly evolving with the current (for reporting best practice) being medallions in a lakehouse. Not that long ago it was all about big data, and of course we currently have AI. As a data leader you need to track and understand these trends but you, or the people you are surrounding yourself with, need the skills and knowledge to know what is sensible for your organisation to try and to adopt. 

## Governance 

It is best not to think of Governance as red tape; it is the "rules of the game" and the "referee on the pitch" that allows the game to flow safely and fairly. I know I have been guilty of getting annoyed at the walls put up throughout organisations but when you dig deeper they are just making sure you are doing the right thing. 

At the time of writing I am very much in the technical space and have governance applied to the work I do rather than being involved in the development of a framework to, well govern. But we use the principles from data governance to ensure the right people have access to the right data, that PII data is stored and used only as required and much more and is something I intend on delving into more detail on.

The key is finding "Goldilocks Governance" - Finding the balanced level of control—not too soft (chaos), not too hard (bureaucracy that drives users back to shadow spreadsheets), but just right. AS Peter and Caroline would have said on the course I just killed a data fairy. Spreadsheets, often used but often the nemesis of data professionals can be a nightmare for data governance too. 

All of this can be lots of fun and games in different organisations. In my past I have been involved in audits and assisting external companies that are ensuring we were meeting regulations and that our changes to code and data were justified and signed off. 

## Closing Notes

Well we also had notes on imposter syndrome. I have been through a lot over the last few years, well I have learnt a lot about new tech, managing people and all of the above. I learnt a lot on this course and have only briefly covered it here to say, go on their course next year and see what you can learn and what lessons you can take away to help yourself and your org.
